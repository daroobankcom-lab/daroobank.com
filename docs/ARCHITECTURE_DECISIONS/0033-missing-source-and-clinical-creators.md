# ADR 0033: current creator-account presence in source and clinical workflows

Status: development-only, 22 November 2026. Narrows the missing-parent-author limitation noted in [ADR 0031](0031-clinical-detail-review-comparisons.md) and [ADR 0032](0032-reviewer-lookup-in-intake-history.md) for the specific source and clinical paths below, not for other domains or ancestry. The clinical scan was subsequently extended by [ADR 0056](0056-clinical-inspection-author-presence.md) and the source queue by [ADR 0057](0057-source-queue-recorder-presence.md).

## Problem and decision

Source claims and clinical drafts have non-null creator FKs, ordinarily protected by PostgreSQL constraints and application deletion rules. In a selected synthetic test with deferred FK corruption, the direct source and clinical review services nevertheless accepted a nonexistent creator ID; an inner join also hid the orphaned source claim or clinical draft in private detail/queue views. This is a corruption/bypass scenario, not the normal committed state under intact FK constraints.

Source review now checks that the current recorded-by ID resolves to a user after locking the claim and validating its payload. Clinical review, supplementary-reference addition and revision creation check the **selected subject draft's** current author after locking it, its immediate parent when needed, and validating the payload pair. A missing account results in the existing conflict path with no write. Authorization still precedes input validation and self-review remains forbidden. The check is existence only: inactive or non-staff creator accounts still exist and are not treated as proof of qualifications or past authorization. Checks add a separate SELECT in each writer, do not lock user rows, and last no longer than the read. The clinical parent author's account and more distant ancestors are not checked.

Remove creator inner joins from source and clinical detail and queues. Source detail shows the unverified claim with an explicit missing-account notice and disables the review form; clinical detail suppresses draft content and write actions when the author is missing. A syntactically valid source or clinical review POST still checks the service and returns conflict. The clinical queue uses a single page-local lookup for at most 20 author IDs and hides the affected draft's text; the source queue does not query creator accounts. Existing date summary, source date inspection, clinical technical scan, clinical lineage and review-inspection classifiers remain payload/date-oriented and do not establish creator-account existence; the date and clinical scan pages explicitly say so. These paths can therefore label an orphan's payload as technically available while its clinical detail blocks writing. No records are changed or repaired.

## Evidence and limitations

Tests use deferred FK corruption, restoring the IDs before the test transaction ends; they verify service conflicts for all four writers, 409/private HTTP responses, orphan visibility in queues, hidden clinical text, existing permissions and normal writes. A 20-draft queue test observes no per-row account queries. Tests confirm that the clinical scan and source date scan do not classify creator presence, and that a child review can proceed while its immediate parent's author account is missing. Current inactive/non-staff accounts pass the existence check. Test-only patches in the **same transaction** demonstrate that a nonparticipating later mutation can still create inconsistent state after the check; a separate-connection PostgreSQL test shows an account can be deactivated while each of the two review operations is paused. This does not demonstrate a concurrent account deletion or every possible schedule.

User accounts are not locked and the read does not guarantee existence at commit or after it, attribution, historical identity, qualification or unchanged privileges. Account IDs are not included in the source or clinical payload hash. Bypass SQL, disabled/deferred constraints, actor changes, absent supplementary references, and later changes remain possible. The other ingestion/identity domains and other ancestor creators are out of scope. Page-local counts, output caps and single queries do not bound SQL work, bytes or runtime. Tests and Chromium cover selected synthetic cases, not clinical approval, source rights, an audit, production hardening or accessibility across devices.
