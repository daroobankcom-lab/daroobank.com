# Phase 1 foundation: evidence and outstanding decisions

This is a working checklist, **not** an approval, a percentage complete, a production runbook, or evidence that patient data may be used. The local application and every test dataset must remain synthetic. The Phase 1 scope and broader dependencies are in [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md); security and operational controls are described in [Security](SECURITY.md) and [Deployment](DEPLOYMENT.md).

**Status key:** `development evidence` means a limited local implementation or exercise exists, not that an operational gate has passed. `open` means a decision or implementation remains. Items can have both statuses. Owners and dates are intentionally not invented: the project must assign them.

| Area | Development evidence | Outstanding gate / evidence to collect before any production authorization |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture and hosting | ADRs and a modular Django/PostgreSQL skeleton exist. | **Open:** owners approve architecture, data classification, provider/region, legal and operational responsibilities; assess connectivity, vendor and data-residency constraints. Document decisions and exceptions. |
| Data model and migrations | Migrations and PostgreSQL-backed synthetic tests exist, and the rollback direction is now measured: on throwaway PostgreSQL databases the suite migrates, rolls back and reapplies 16 migrations across six apps, requires each app to be one linear chain, requires every data operation to declare an explicit reverse and names the two whose reverse is a no-op, derives the cascade of a single-app rollback from the migration graph (rolling catalog back unapplies exactly 11 migrations, its own eight plus pricing's two and ingestion's one, and leaves no foreign key without its target), and requires the column, index and constraint inventory after a full rollback and reapply to be identical to the first migration — 32 tables, 276 columns, 108 indexes, 136 constraints, 50 foreign keys and 31 applied rows on a freshly migrated database, checked against the same model-derived expectations CI applies to the development database. `makemigrations --check --dry-run` also runs inside the suite, mirroring the CI step (ADR 0216). | **Open:** a staging upgrade and rollback **plan** — environment, order, backup and restore step, owner and authorization for a destructive operation. Every rollback above ran against an empty database, so nothing here shows a rollback with data in it; Django drops tables and columns without preserving their contents. Rollback of Django's own apps, lock behavior on a populated database, and tested recovery from a real staging schema are unmeasured. |
| API and permissions | Versioned OpenAPI and selected server-side authorization tests exist, plus a route-by-route staff authorization matrix over 41 `staff_*` routes and six synthetic account shapes, repeated against real rows for the 23 object routes so a denial cannot become an existence oracle, with a coverage assertion tied to the live URL resolver (ADR 0200–0201). Every non-staff route is likewise inventoried from the resolver and probed for private-data leakage with two positive controls (ADR 0202), and the same staff matrix is repeated for group grants, an active superuser, a superuser without `is_staff`, a deactivated account and an alternate authentication backend (ADR 0203). Denials for existing and missing rows are also compared byte-for-byte in status, body and headers, so a route cannot leak existence through content (ADR 0204), and the same routes are probed with thirteen identifier shapes and both method directions so a malformed identifier or a wrong method cannot be distinguished per account (ADR 0205), and nine caller-controlled query shapes across all 41 staff routes cannot change a denial, cause a server error or widen a kind filter (ADR 0206). The CSRF gate is measured across all 43 staff routes: a token-less post yields one canonical 403 for five account shapes, five token failure modes are indistinguishable, and with a valid token each route answers exactly as its view decorators declare (ADR 0207). Logout, cookie replay, a password change and forced session expiry are each walked across all 41 routes behind a positive control (ADR 0208); the open blocker in ADR 0191 is unchanged by this. Six HTTP methods are swept across all 43 staff routes, pinning that no destructive method is accepted, that `Allow` advertises only declared methods, and that a method rejection is identical for every account shape (ADR 0209). The whole header surface of those routes is then pinned: across 1 204 responses (43 routes × 7 methods × 4 accounts) seven privacy headers must appear with exact values, `Allow` only where a method was refused, `text/csv` and `Content-Disposition` only on the two sample downloads, and no header at all outside a closed inventory (ADR 0210). Redirect targets are probed with 21 caller-supplied `next` values across both authentication endpoints and all 41 routes: a hostile value never becomes a target, an anonymous redirect always lands on the login page with the requested staff URL as its return target, and the login form echoes only the validated value (ADR 0211). The whole inventory is collected in [the authorization review](AUTHORIZATION_REVIEW.md), where both tables are generated from the live resolver and the pinned suites rather than typed, a drift test re-renders them, and every cited ADR is checked to exist (ADR 0215). `django.contrib.admin` is deliberately not installed — the roadmap's "auth/admin baseline" is the hand-built portal — and seven tests pin that absence: the installed-app list, seven admin paths that do not resolve, a byte-identical 179-byte 404 for an anonymous caller and a superuser, no `django_admin_log` table, no project import of the admin, no third surface in the resolver, and the five operational commands that replace admin screens (ADR 0218). | **Open:** the review's *judgement* — approve each route's requirement, approve exposure and permissions for the intended deployment, and add threat modeling and an independent security assessment. The review document lists these as unanswered questions with blank owner and date columns; nothing in it is a sign-off. |
| Staff authentication | Debug-only staff portal and opt-in synthetic IP-window limiter exist; duplicate login fields are rejected. Sixteen guarded write services across fourteen staff routes (source-claim creation and review, price review, clinical review, product review, product and price staging, clinical draft create/revise and reference create, and the seven identity workspace writes) recheck account flags, permissions and the session password hash inside their transactions through one shared helper, and all fourteen staff write routes have synchronized in-flight tests plus a route inventory drift guard (ADR 0192–0199). Sessions are now bound at login to a digest of the account state and a middleware ends a session whose account differs from it, read from the account the session names rather than from `request.user`, so deactivation, staff-flag removal, permission and group changes, account deletion and password changes delete the session row and restoring the state does not revive it; a bounded development-only command revokes the sessions of one account or all of them for the transition no request observes, and a read-only inventory reports, per account, which sessions exist, which are unbound and which the guard would end — listing aliases rather than session keys (ADR 0213–0214). | **Open:** MFA enrollment and recovery, replay protection for a stolen cookie whose account state never changes, an approved production revocation and inventory procedure with access control, audit and an owner, production-grade account and network abuse controls, proxy identity, alerting and independently reviewed policy. The current limiter is not a production control, and the guarded-write rechecks cover sixteen services and selected orderings, not other writes, permission races, the unlocked rechecks, preview or actor-less staging. The session guard fires only when a request arrives, so an off/on transition that no request observes leaves a dormant session usable until it is revoked explicitly, and removing an authentication backend from settings is not observable at request time. See the unapproved [MFA implementation plan](MFA_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md), the [session revocation record](ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS/0191-staff-session-revocation-gap.md) and [ADR 0213](ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS/0213-durable-session-invalidation.md). |
| Secrets and access | Non-debug startup checks selected secret and host shapes; runtime dependencies are pinned. | **Open:** secret manager, key generation/rotation and recovery, least-privilege accounts and access review. Shape checks do not demonstrate entropy or secrecy. |
| Network and TLS | Optional proxy-scheme trust and HSTS settings exist; both are off by default. | **Open:** actual network isolation, trusted proxy configuration and header sanitization, approved hostnames, TLS issuance/renewal, HTTPS/CSRF/cookie checks and HSTS rollback plan; see ADRs 0184–0185. |
| Backups and recovery | Synthetic PostgreSQL dump/restore and encrypted-envelope negative controls exercise a limited local scenario. | **Open:** encrypted off-site backups, independent key custody, access control, retention, integrity and anti-replay/freshness policy, recovery objectives, scheduled isolated restore drills and documented owner. The synthetic test is not an operating backup service. |
| Monitoring and response | Minimal opt-in process and PostgreSQL probes exist, and `manage.py check_deployment_readiness` now collects the wider shape into one read-only JSON report: ten required checks (debug off, staff portal off, secret and host shape, secure session and CSRF cookies, the session flags ADR 0208 pins, HTTPS redirection, no pending migrations, no applied migration without a file in this release, and a reachable database) plus a separate list of conditions that need a decision rather than a fix, with `--strict` failing on those too. Every required check is measured to be able to fail on its own, the report never contains the secret value, two runs of one configuration are byte-identical, and the command writes nothing (ADR 0217). All fifteen environment variables the application reads are now inventoried from the code and documented per audience, including a deployment configuration reference that states the production expectation for each (ADR 0222). What is not monitored is measured too: the application records no read at all — `LOGGING` is empty, no module imports `logging`, no middleware records a request, no table can hold an access record, and sweeping all 55 routes creates no row (ADR 0221) — so any request history has to come from the proxy, the database or the host. | **Open:** private access restrictions, network/request deadlines, monitoring, alert routing, on-call ownership and incident response. The readiness report describes one process's settings and one database connection in the environment that runs it: no request is served, no static-file, logging, clock, pooling, replica or external-dependency condition is checked, and it has never been run against a real deployment, so "production-specific" readiness is still unproven. Probe success proves neither migrations nor recovery, and a zero exit status is not a deployment authorization. |
| CI and supply chain | GitHub Actions runs development tests and selected dependency scans. | **Open:** independent review, immutable production artifacts and image digests, OS/transitive dependency review, staging/release gates and rollback. CI success is not a security certification. |
| Data and clinical governance | Only synthetic workflows and selected validation tests exist. Actor attribution is measured rather than assumed: 19 of the 23 project models name the account that wrote them behind a protected, mostly required foreign key plus a creation timestamp, and no model records modification; the published catalog record names nobody and no management command records its operator. | **Open:** qualified legal/privacy and clinical governance, source rights, retention, protected audit and explicit publication approval. No clinical or pricing accuracy is established here. |

## How to close a gate

1. Assign an accountable owner, scope, decision deadline and applicable environment; link an approved policy or decision record.
2. Capture dated, reproducible evidence and exceptions without placing secrets, patient data or sensitive infrastructure details in this repository.
3. Have the relevant independent security, operations, legal or clinical reviewer approve the evidence where applicable. A successful local test is supporting evidence only.
4. Obtain explicit authorization before provisioning or deploying production infrastructure, importing real data, or publishing clinical/price claims. Record rollback and incident ownership separately.

A working development control must not be promoted to a passed operational gate merely because it appears in this checklist. See [Testing strategy](TESTING_STRATEGY.md) for exact test scope and known gaps.
